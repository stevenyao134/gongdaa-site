PVC Film Quality Testing: A Factory Acceptance Checklist

Published: November 18, 2026  |  By GONGDA Team

Every film programme eventually produces the same argument: the buyer says the film arrived wrong, the supplier says it left the factory right, and neither side has a checklist either can point to. A written receiving-inspection standard ends that argument before it starts - and it is the single cheapest quality investment a joinery or panel line can make.

Here is the checklist we recommend, in the order a receiving bay should run it.

1. Documentation First

Inspect the paperwork before the pallet, because the documents define what the goods should be:

  1. Design reference code matches the order, on the roll label and the packing list.
  2. Batch number present on every roll - no batch number, no traceability, no claim.
  3. Third-party compliance reports naming the product: REACH, RoHS 2.0, phthalate limits. Ours are SGS-tested and published on the certifications page.
  4. Any agreed performance data for the specific batch - UV ageing, abrasion - attached, not promised.

2. Visual Inspection

Unroll one full revolution of a roll from each batch under good light, at an angle:

  • Print defects: missing pattern, banding, ink specks, colour drift against the master sample.
  • Emboss consistency - raking light exposes gloss patches and texture skips.
  • Surface contamination: dust spots, oil marks, creases.
  • Roll condition: dents, flat spots, damaged packaging - all feed problems later.

3. Dimensions

CheckMethodWhat you are catching
ThicknessMicrometer at several points along the rollGauge drift - feeds unevenly, laminates unevenly
WidthTape across the roll face, slit edges includedSlit-width error; burred edges
Roll lengthMark and rewind count or weigh against declared linear weightShort rolls - the classic silent shortfall

The declared linear weight is the fastest integrity check: weigh the roll, divide by the declared grams per metre, and any real shortfall shows immediately.

Gauge consistency is born at the calender - and verified at your receiving bay.

4. Colour and Gloss Against the Master

Compare against the sealed master sample - the one agreed before the order, dated, and archived - under a defined light source, not whatever lamp hangs over the dock. Gloss drift reads to the eye as colour drift, so measure both if a gloss meter is available. This is the step that prevents the batch-3-does-not-match-batch-1 argument described in our office furniture and wardrobe guides.

5. Trial on Your Own Line

The test that matters most is the one only you can run:

  1. Laminate a sample panel with your own adhesive, pressure and settings - the supplier's line is not yours.
  2. Check bond by cut-back after full cure, at the edges and corners first.
  3. Run the film through your profiling or cutting to see chip behaviour.
  4. Keep the trial panel as the reference for every future delivery of that design.

The trial panel is the contract between supplier film and your line - keep it archived.

6. Performance Expectations Worth Asking About

  • UV and colour fastness: our soft-touch surfaces, for example, are tested to ISO 4892-3 UVA-340 for 72 hours with grey scale 4 to 5 - ask for the equivalent data on any premium finish, and read the finishes guide for why finish choice moves these numbers.
  • Abrasion: relevant for touch-height surfaces; ask how it was tested, not just the number.
  • Fire performance: belongs to the assembly, per the fire guide - do not accept a film-only claim.

A checklist only works if failures have a path: quarantine the batch, photograph with the batch label visible, and send the panel and the roll section to the supplier. Supplier vetting - twelve red flags before you sign - is in the vetting guide, and the buyers-guide framing for the whole evaluation is in the buyers guide.
