PVC Film for Retail and Hospitality Interiors: A Fit-Out Guide

Published: November 18, 2026  |  By GONGDA Team

Retail and hospitality fit-outs are where decorative film earns its boldest brief: a forty-metre marble-look reception front, a flagship store refit between lease dates, a hotel corridor programme across ten floors. The surfaces are big, the traffic is brutal, and the schedule never has slack.

It is also the segment where film specification is most often copied from a mood board without the practical clauses - which is why this guide runs from zone selection through durability to the fire clause.

Zones and What Belongs in Each

ZoneFilm choiceThe reason
Feature walls and backdropsBook-match marble or metallicMaximum visual impact; the pairing logic is in our marble and metallic guide
Reception desks and bar frontsHeavy-gauge film, matched edges, matte to silkHorizontal, touched all day; durability outranks drama
Retail fixtures and shelvingWood grains with matched banding, silk finishVolume production, cost per fixture, clean cut quality
Corridors and guest room joinerySuper-matte wood grains, robust gaugeTrolley scuffs and luggage strikes; matte hides them
Ceilings and column wrapsStandard gauge, any finishUntouched surfaces - carry the design for free

Fixture programmes live on cut quality and edge match - the joinery does the talking.

Durability and Cleaning in Traffic

Hospitality surfaces are cleaned daily with whatever the housekeeping cupboard holds. Specify for it:

  • Matte and super-matte finishes survive chemical cleaners and trolley scuffs far better than gloss; gloss belongs on touched-by-design surfaces only.
  • Soft-touch at hand height - door pushes, lift buttons surrounds, counter noses - resists fingerprints; the care boundaries are in the durability and maintenance guide.
  • Write the cleaning regime into handover - the film survives anything except abrasive pads on gloss, and housekeeping should know that in writing.

Multi-site rollouts are decided by batch consistency - one master sample, one mill, one gloss standard.

The Fire Clause, Handled Properly

Hospitality tenders carry fire clauses - corridor linings, escape routes, assembly occupancies. The clause is real; the handling of it is where projects go wrong. The honest position, set out fully in our fire ratings guide: the rating belongs to the finished assembly of core and facing, tested as built. Ask for the core supplier's report, ask us for the film's chemistry documentation - REACH, RoHS 2.0 and phthalate reports by SGS - and specify the assembly, not an adjective.

Multi-Site Rollouts

The retail chain refit is the hardest consistency test in the business: forty stores, six months, three installation crews. What makes it work:

  1. One sealed master per design, archived and referenced for the whole rollout.
  2. Batch gloss values recorded and published to the fit-out manager.
  3. Roll quantity per site planned with overage agreed before production - retrofitting a match after the fact is the expensive failure.
  4. Panel numbering for book-match walls agreed as a drawing, not improvised on site.

For the wall systems themselves - substrates, panel formats and installation - the wall panel guide carries the construction detail. And where the programme needs the look changed quickly in leased space, the removable-grade route in the self-adhesive guide is worth a line in the plan.
