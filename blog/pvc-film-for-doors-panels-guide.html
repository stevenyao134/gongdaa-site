PVC Film for Doors and Door Panels: A Practical Guide

Published: November 18, 2026  |  By GONGDA Team

The interior door is the most-closed surface in any building and the least examined in the specification. Programmes that treat doors as leftover film from the joinery order end up with the visible failures - edge whitening, profile delamination, skin show-through - that door buyers actually inspect. The film route follows the door construction, and door construction has three families.

Route 1: Flat Laminated Leaves

Flush and panel-look doors built from flat cores - particleboard, MDF, honeycomb with facings - laminated with decorative film on flat lamination lines. This is the volume route:

  • Gauge follows the facing. Skins and facings carry 0.12 to 0.25mm comfortably; a heavy flush leaf finished in solid colour takes more, per the thickness guide logic.
  • Wood grains dominate. Doors are the timber-look application - synchronised embossing earns its premium here because hands and eyes meet the door daily.
  • Both faces matter. A door has two visible sides and six edges; specify the reverse face finish deliberately, not as an accident of stock.

Door detail lives at the edges - lock side, hinge side and top are where programmes are judged.

Route 2: HDF Door Skins

Moulded HDF skins pressed in volume, laminated with film before or after forming. This is the highest-volume film application in the door industry, and the discipline is consistency: one design, one gauge, millions of square feet. Skin laminating rewards film with tight gauge tolerance and clean cut behaviour on the profiling line - the same properties that matter in the receiving checklist.

Route 3: Milled Thermofoil Doors

Routed MDF doors wrapped by vacuum membrane pressing - the seamless route for profiled and panel-look doors. The grade logic is covered completely in the membrane foil guide; the door-specific notes:

  • Deep rail-and-field profiles need deep-draw grades - match grade to the deepest profile, not the average.
  • Lock-edge and hinge-edge returns are where wrap quality shows; include them in the trial door.
  • Five-side wraps leave the bottom edge as a decision - specify it, because installers will discover it either way.

The lock edge is the most-handled edge on the door - finish and bond there deserve their own QC point.

Moisture Is the Door Story

Doors meet water where other joinery does not: bathroom door edges, kitchen swings, mopping regimes, storage before install. Film faces this better than most finishes - PVC is waterproof by chemistry - but the bond line is not the film:

  1. Seal cut edges before installation where the spec allows - the board, not the film, is the wick.
  2. Bathroom and kitchen doors: specify the adhesive and edge system for humidity duty; the same logic as our edge adhesive guide.
  3. Storage and site handling keep door packs dry - a swollen door defeats any finish.

Specification Lines for Door Programmes

  1. Name the route per door type - leaf, skin or membrane - in one schedule.
  2. Match edges and film to one master sample; doors and frames must agree.
  3. State the reverse-face and edge finish per door type.
  4. Require batch traceability - door programmes run for years across phases.
  5. Include the compliance file with the quote: REACH, RoHS 2.0, phthalates, documented per product.

Doors sit next to the hardest-working surfaces in the building - the kitchen cabinetry beside them is covered in the kitchen cabinets guide, and the wardrobe doors they mirror are in the wardrobe guide. Specify all three from one master set and the whole interior reads as one mill's work.
