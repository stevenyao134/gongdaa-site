Marble and Metallic PVC Film: What to Check Before You Specify

Published: November 10, 2026  |  By GONGDA Team

Marble and metallic PVC film sells the most expensive-looking surfaces in the range for a fraction of the material cost of the real thing. It is also the category where the most samples get approved and the most panels get rejected - because veining direction, metallic shimmer and book-match alignment all behave differently on a 100mm swatch than they do across a 2.4 metre feature wall.

This guide explains how the finishes are actually made, where each one belongs, what the specification limits are, and the four checks that keep a marble or metallic programme from falling apart between sample approval and installation.

How the Finishes Are Made

Neither look is an overlay or a foil laminate. Both are printed and embossed into the PVC film itself, which is why they survive lamination, wrapping and vacuum pressing like any other design in the range.

  • Marble finishes are built from multi-station printing of translucent veining over a stone base tone, followed by texture embossing that follows the vein pattern. Depth comes from the emboss, not from a gel layer - which is why a well-made marble film reads as stone even in raking light.
  • Metallic finishes use metallic-effect ink systems to build brushed, mirrored and oxidised-metal looks. Brushed metal needs the print direction held consistently across the whole run; that direction discipline is what separates a convincing brushed surface from one that visibly changes character between panels.
  • Book-matched marble is produced so that adjacent panels mirror each other, the way a real slab cut and opened does. It is the single highest-impact option for feature walls and reception fronts.

Veining is printed in translucent layers over the stone base tone, then embossed to follow the pattern.

The Specification Limits That Matter

Marble and metallic designs run across the standard decorative film range: gauges from 0.12mm to 0.45mm on jumbo roll widths of 1,000 to 1,400mm, slit to width for the line. Two specification notes are specific to this category:

  • Gauge follows the pattern. Pale marbles and metallics sit on the opacity-sensitive end of the range - a light Calacatta over a dark MDF will telegraph the substrate at 0.12mm. Our thickness guide covers the trade-offs; as a rule, light stone looks start at 0.25mm.
  • Book-match needs panel-pair planning. Veining alignment only works if the installation honours the pairing. Panels must be slit from matched positions and installed in sequence, which means the quantity and the panel numbering should be agreed before production, not improvised on site.

A marble front is judged at the edge - matched banding from the same design reference closes the look.

Matching the Edge

A marble or metallic front fails visually at the edge if the banding does not carry the same veining. Our edge banding is extruded from the same colour match as the film, from 0.4 to 3mm, so the pattern continues around the panel. For book-match programmes, the edge should be matched to the film before the panel is cut - retrofitting an edge that almost-matches is the detail customers photograph and send back.

The Four Checks Before You Specify

  1. Sample the actual design, full size if possible. Veining direction and scale look completely different on screen than on the roll, so order a sample before specifying - this is the check our own product page leads with, because it is the one that prevents the most disputes.
  2. Approve the book-match layout as a drawing. Mark panel positions and pairing on an elevation before production. A sample pair is not a layout.
  3. Check the emboss in raking light. Tilt the sample under a low light source: this is how the finished wall will be seen, and it exposes both emboss quality and gloss uniformity that straight-on viewing hides.
  4. Confirm the compliance file. Whatever the finish, the third-party reports should name the product - ours are tested by SGS against REACH, RoHS 2.0 and phthalate limits, published in full on the certifications page.

Maintenance Reality

Marble-effect matte and low-sheen finishes clean like any decorative film: soft cloth, mild cleaner, no abrasives. The surfaces that need honest warnings are the gloss end - high-gloss marble and mirror metallic show fingerprints and micro-scratches that a matte finish hides. In hospitality and retail, that maintenance difference decides the specification more often than the design does.

Between the marble and wood-grain families, the choice is usually made by the interior concept rather than the material - but the practical differences are real, and our comparison of the two families sets out where each one earns its place.
