PVC Decorative Film Thickness: Choosing Between 0.12mm and 0.45mm

Published: October 11, 2026  |  By GONGDA Team

Thickness is the specification buyers quote first and understand least. It is easy to compare, it sounds like a quality indicator, and it is the single easiest place for a supplier to look generous while changing nothing that matters. A 0.45mm film is not a better film than a 0.25mm film. It is a different film for a different job.

This article sets out what each gauge in our range is actually for, what thickness genuinely changes, and the three places where specifying thicker is simply money spent for no return.

The Range and What Each Gauge Is For

Our decorative film is produced in five standard gauges. Each one exists because a specific process or market needs it.

ThicknessWhat it is forTypical process
0.12mmEconomical flat-lamination film where the panel is fully covered and the substrate is smoothFlat lamination, cold or hot roll
0.18mmThe general-purpose gauge - the largest share of furniture and cabinet volumeFlat lamination, wrapping, membrane pressing
0.25mmBetter opacity and edge concealment; the practical starting point for patterned marble and metallic designsLamination, membrane pressing, profile wrapping
0.35mmFormed and profiled parts, and surfaces that must hide substrate textureMembrane pressing, profile wrapping, 3D lamination
0.45mmDeep-draw forming and heavy-wear surfaces; also used where a thicker edge profile must not show throughDeep-draw membrane pressing, post-forming

Roll length runs 200 to 500m depending on gauge and customer requirement, in jumbo roll widths of 1,000 to 1,400mm with custom slitting.

Gauge is set at the calender, so it is a production decision rather than a coating added later.

What Thickness Actually Changes

Five things move when you change gauge, and only some of them are things you want to move.

  • Opacity and hiding power. Real and useful. A thicker film covers a darker or more variable substrate with less show-through, which matters most on light solid colours and pale marbles.
  • Stiffness and handling. Thicker film is easier to feed flat and less prone to creasing during lamination, but harder to wrap around tight radii and returns.
  • Draw depth in vacuum pressing. Deep-draw work needs a grade built for elongation - which is why our membrane foil is offered in 0.25, 0.30, 0.35 and 0.45mm rather than the thinnest gauges.
  • Edge concealment. Thicker film hides a slightly proud substrate edge better. It does not fix a badly trimmed one - and the joint itself is a bonding question, covered in our edge banding adhesive guide.
  • Weight, roll count and freight. This is the one buyers forget. Doubling the gauge roughly doubles the weight per metre, which halves the roll count a container will carry. That changes your landed cost before it changes anything else.

Three Things Thickness Does Not Fix

1. Surface wear resistance

Abrasion and scratch performance come from the topcoat and the embossing, not the total gauge. Two films of the same thickness from different suppliers can differ substantially in how they survive a kitchen. Specify the abrasion requirement, and let the supplier decide which gauge and topcoat combination meets it.

2. Adhesion

If the film lifts at a wrapped edge, thicker film will lift with more force. Adhesion is a question of glue type, pressing temperature, substrate moisture content and cycle time. Buying a heavier gauge to solve a bond problem is one of the most common and most expensive mistakes in this category.

3. Colour accuracy

Colour comes from the ink system and the pigment, not the film weight. A thicker film with a thin or poorly controlled ink lay will still drift between batches.

Matching Gauge to Process

Working backwards from the process is usually faster than working forwards from the specification sheet.

ProcessPractical gaugeWhy
Flat lamination on a smooth panel0.12 - 0.18mmFull coverage means opacity is not the limiting factor; thin film saves material and freight
Wrapping and profile wrapping0.18 - 0.25mmNeeds enough body to resist tearing at radii without being too stiff to conform
Vacuum membrane pressing (flat to shallow)0.18 - 0.25mmEnough elongation and enough substance to survive the cycle
Vacuum membrane pressing (deep draw)0.25 - 0.45mm membrane gradeDraw depth is limited by elongation, and deep-draw grades are built for it
High-traffic surfaces, light colours0.25 - 0.35mmOpacity and edge concealment matter more than material saving

Cabinet fronts are where the gauge decision is most visible and least forgiving.

Where Thickness Sits in the Cost Stack

Film is priced by area, so thickness does not change the price per square metre the way buyers expect. What it changes is the weight, and weight is what you pay for at the port. Doubling the gauge roughly doubles the weight of the same order, which halves the number of rolls a container carries. On a long-haul shipment the freight consequence of an unnecessarily heavy gauge can exceed the material difference.

That is the argument for specifying the thinnest gauge that genuinely meets the requirement, and for asking the supplier to justify a recommendation rather than simply quote what you asked for. Gauge is one block of the price; the other four are covered here.

A Selection Checklist

  1. Name the process first - lamination, wrapping or pressing - and let it set the gauge range.
  2. Name the abrasion and cleaning expectation, and specify a performance level rather than a thickness.
  3. State the substrate colour and texture, because that decides the opacity you need.
  4. Confirm the roll width and length against your line, since wider does not always mean less waste.
  5. Run a trial in the gauge you intend to order. A 0.18mm sample will not tell you how 0.35mm behaves.

Get the gauge right and everything downstream - adhesion, edge quality, freight - becomes easier. Get it wrong and no amount of process tuning recovers the run.
