PVC Film for Office Furniture: A Specification Guide

Published: November 18, 2026  |  By GONGDA Team

Office furniture is the most specification-driven segment in the decorative film market - and the least forgiving of inconsistency. A hospitality project forgives a slight shade shift between batches; an open-plan office with forty identical desk modules in sight of each other does not. Programmes here are won on batch discipline and lost on it.

This guide covers where film earns its place in office programmes, the finish logic by surface height, the consistency requirements that decide repeat orders, and what to put into the tender.

Where PVC Film Fits in Office Programmes

Desk-height surfaces live or die on fingerprint and wear behaviour - finish choice outranks design choice there.

Batch Consistency Is the Product

The specification question that matters most in office work is not which design - it is whether delivery batch 3 will match delivery batch 1 when the fit-out runs over six months. Our answer, and what to demand from any supplier:

  • Colour held against a sealed master for the life of the programme, not per order.
  • Gloss level measured and recorded per batch - gloss drift reads as colour drift to the eye.
  • Batch traceability on every roll, so a site query resolves in hours.
  • Over-order policy agreed up front - hold capacity or stock for the programme rather than re-matching after the fact.

Finish Logic by Surface Height

Office surfaces fail by touch, so finish selection follows hand height. Desks, armrests and anything at standing-desk height take soft-touch or silk with anti-fingerprint topcoats - the maintenance behaviour is documented in our soft-touch guide. Wall-hung and high surfaces can carry gloss or texture freely. Floor-adjacent surfaces - modesty panels, credenza ends - take matte wood grains that forgive scuffs.

The panel line rewards films that laminate flat and cut clean - gloss demands the flattest cores.

What Goes Into the Tender

  1. Name the design by reference code and the physical master sample, sealed and dated.
  2. State gauge per surface type - horizontal desk surfaces carry more gauge than vertical screens; the logic is in our thickness guide.
  3. Require the compliance file: REACH, RoHS 2.0 and phthalate reports naming the product - ours are published on the certifications page.
  4. Specify batch colour tolerance and the master sample as the arbiter.
  5. Match the edge banding reference to the film in the same clause - office fronts are judged at the edge.

Office programmes also surface the documentation questions - fire clauses, emissions, tender scoring - that we treat in detail in the fire ratings guide and the sustainability guide. The suppliers who win repeat office work are the ones whose paperwork answers those clauses before being asked.
