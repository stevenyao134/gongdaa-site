PVC Film Lamination Defects: Causes and Fixes That Last

Published: November 18, 2026  |  By GONGDA Team

Lamination defects repeat for one reason: the fix addressed the symptom, not the cause. A blister sanded and re-laminated returns, because the moisture that made it never left the building. This guide walks the five defects that account for most flat-lamination rejects, traces each to its usual root, and gives the fix that actually holds.

Defect 1: Blisters and Bubbles

Localised lifting of the film from the panel, appearing immediately or within days.

Usual causesThe fix that lasts
Panel moisture - steam forms under the film and lifts itStore panels in condition; equilibrate freshly machined panels before laminating
Adhesive skip or starvation in patchesVerify spread rate and roller condition; map coverage on the adhesive roll, not the panel
Trapped air between film and adhesiveCheck nip pressure and roll alignment; degas two-component adhesives properly

The moisture cause is the most misdiagnosed: it shows in seasons, clusters in freshly delivered board, and disappears when someone 'fixes' the adhesive - until next spring. Moisture content discipline at board intake is the permanent fix.

Defect 2: Delamination at Edges and Profiles

Bond failure that starts at cut edges or routed profiles and creeps inward.

Usual causesThe fix that lasts
Adhesive quantity too low at the perimeterCorrect spread rate overall; pay attention to edge coverage specifically
Open time exceeded - adhesive skinned before the film landedMatch line speed to the adhesive's open time; shorten the film-to-press interval
Insufficient pressure or temperature at the nipRe-verify settings against the adhesive datasheet after any maintenance
Contaminated topcoat or dusty board facesClean handling between machining and laminating - dust is invisible until the bond fails

Temperature discipline at the nip and through cooling decides whether the bond polymerises or merely sticks.

Defect 3: Orange Peel and Glossy Spots

Surface texture that should not exist - dimpling, or random glossier patches on a matte face. Causes sit almost entirely upstream of the film:

  • Orange peel usually traces to adhesive applied too thick and levelling poorly, showing through thin film gauges - reduce spread or move up a gauge, per the thickness guide.
  • Glossy spots on matte film trace to burnishing: rollers or conveyors with hardened residue polishing patches of the surface. Clean the line; the film was fine.
  • Random shiny freckles can also be roller-embedded debris - a line maintenance issue, not a film issue.

Defect 4: Telegraphing

The substrate showing through the face - core waves, sanding marks, grain raise. The film reproduces the panel; it cannot hide it. On gloss finishes this is severe and structural: a mirror-finish front audits the core. The fix is board selection and sanding discipline upstream, plus finish choice where the core grade cannot be improved - the logic is in the finishes guide.

Gauge consistency from the calender is what lets a thin film laminate without showing the adhesive beneath.

Defect 5: Print Bleed and Colour Migration

Ink appearing to move, blur or mark the overlay after laminating. True ink migration on a properly cured film is rare; what presents as bleed is usually:

  • Plasticiser interaction with an incompatible adhesive chemistry - check the adhesive is rated for PVC film faces.
  • Overheating at the nip softening the ink layer - verify the temperature window against the film datasheet.
  • Contamination transfer from the press platen or previous adhesive residue on the belt - clean before blaming chemistry.

The Discipline That Prevents All Five

  1. Incoming QC per batch - the checklist in our quality testing guide.
  2. One change at a time: changing film batch, adhesive and settings together guarantees the cause is never found.
  3. Keep a defect log with photos, batch numbers and line settings - patterns emerge across weeks, not shifts.
  4. Retain a sample of any suspect batch for the supplier to analyse.

Most 'film problems' are process problems wearing a film costume - but when the film is the cause, gauge consistency and topcoat discipline are what separate suppliers, and those are exactly the stages described in our production guide.
