PVC Membrane Foil for Cabinet Doors: Grades, Draw Depth and Quality Checks

Published: November 10, 2026  |  By GONGDA Team

Thermofoil doors are the reason milled MDF became a cabinet industry workhorse. A single vacuum press cycle wraps the face, the profile and the return edges in one continuous surface - no seams, no edge banding line, no lacquer booth. The enabling material is membrane foil, and the difference between a door programme that runs for years and one that comes back in warranty is almost entirely grade selection and pressing discipline.

This guide covers what membrane foil is, how the standard and deep-draw grades differ, which finish families the market wants, and the press defects a quality check has to catch before the doors ship.

What Membrane Foil Is

Membrane foil is a printed, embossed PVC film formulated for vacuum membrane pressing: the door - usually routed MDF, sometimes engineered substrate - is coated with adhesive, the foil is laid over it, a flexible membrane presses down, and vacuum draws the foil into every routed profile while heat activates the bond. One cycle produces a five-side wrapped door with a seamless painted-look or grained surface.

That seamless wrap is the product. A profiled door assembled from flat film and edge banding shows a joint at every profile change; a membrane-pressed door shows none. This is why thermofoil owns the milled-door segment and why flat-panel programmes use different products.

The Two Grade Families: Standard Stretch and Deep Draw

GradeElongationRight forWrong for
Standard stretchModerate - reliable on shallow profilesShaker doors, simple routed panels, shallow grooves, flat fronts with edge returnsDeep mouldings, tight internal corners, complex 3D shapes
Deep drawHigh - up to 250% elongationDeep routed profiles, raised mouldings, complex 3D wardrobe and kitchen frontsEconomical simple panels - you pay for capability the door does not use

Elongation is the property that decides the job. When the foil draws into a deep profile, it must stretch without thinning past the point where the print fades or the film whitens at the corners. A deep-draw grade on a shallow door wastes money; a standard grade on a deep moulding produces thin corners and rejects. Match the grade to the measured draw depth of the deepest profile in the programme - not to the average one.

Finish Families the Market Asks For

  • Super-matte and soft-touch carry the premium wardrobe and kitchen programmes - the velvet, anti-fingerprint surfaces that photograph as luxury. Our soft-touch films are tested for abrasion and UV ageing, and the finish story is covered in detail in our soft-touch guide.
  • High-gloss thermofoil delivers the lacquered look without lacquer cost, solvent emissions or a spray booth. Gloss foils need clean-room discipline in pressing - a speck under a gloss surface is visible from across the room.
  • Wood grain and painted-look solids cover the volume. Synchronised embossing, where the texture registers to the printed grain, is the upgrade that makes a routed grain door read as timber.

Grade and finish are chosen together: the draw depth rules out grades, the market position rules in finishes.

Routing and Substrate Practice

The foil reproduces the substrate exactly, including its mistakes. The practice that matters:

  • Sharp tooling. Dull routers tear MDF fibres, and every torn fibre prints through the foil as surface roughness. Replace profile tooling on a schedule, not on complaint.
  • Clean, dry panels. Moisture in the panel becomes steam in the press, and steam makes blisters. Panels should leave the dust extraction and rest to equilibrate before coating.
  • Even adhesive coverage. Missed adhesive shows as a delamination patch exactly where the profile draws hardest - the corners. Coverage discipline at the corners is what separates a stable door from a warranty return.
  • Dust-free handling. A speck between adhesive and foil is permanent. Panel handling between routing and pressing should be as controlled as the press itself.

Press Defects and What Causes Them

DefectUsual causePrevention
Blisters under the surfacePanel moisture or overheated adhesiveDry and equilibrate panels; hold the adhesive temperature window
Thin, whitened cornersGrade elongation exceeded by the profile drawMove up to a deep-draw grade for that profile
Delamination at profilesAdhesive skip or contamination at cornersVerify coverage before pressing; treat corner coverage as the checkpoint
Gloss specks and orange-peelDirty membranes, dust, wrong cycleClean membrane discipline; press cycle per the foil datasheet
Print fade on deep drawsOver-stretch thinning the ink layerDeep-draw grade with adequate elongation reserve

Foil consistency batch to batch is what makes a pressing programme repeatable.

Quality Checks Before Shipment

  1. Inspect every door's corners under raking light - corners are where every cause of failure shows first.
  2. Run a bond check on a sample door per batch: a cut-back test at the deepest profile, not just at the flat face.
  3. Compare doors from the same batch side by side for gloss level and colour; membrane foil batches should be matched like paint batches.
  4. Check the back face: a five-side wrap leaves the back finish as a deliberate decision, not an accident.
  5. Keep one retained door per batch as the reference for any later claim.

The pressing settings themselves - temperatures, vacuum hold time, membrane care - are covered step by step in our vacuum pressing guide, and the bond system behind a wrapped edge shares its logic with the edge banding adhesive guide. Get the grade and the discipline right, and thermofoil doors carry a premium finish at volume economics.
