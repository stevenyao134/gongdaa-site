How to Write an RFQ That Gets Accurate PVC Film Quotes

Published: November 18, 2026  |  By GONGDA Team

A vague RFQ gets a vague price - and the vagueness is discovered after the purchase order, when the film arrives at the wrong width for the line or the price climbs with every clarification. The quotes that come back comparable, complete and honest are the ones the RFQ earned.

Here is what to put in an RFQ for decorative film and edge banding, field by field, with the reason each field changes your price.

The Twelve Fields That Matter

FieldWrite it like thisWhy it moves the price
Design referenceSupplier design code, or Pantone + physical sample referenceStock designs price one way; custom matching carries development cost
GaugeOne gauge per application, not a rangeGauge is the largest single cost driver - see the thickness guide
WidthSlit width or jumbo roll widthSlitting adds a stage; jumbo rolls price better
Roll length / packagingMetres per roll and rolls per cartonPackaging format changes both cost and freight
Application methodFlat lamination / wrapping / vacuum membrane pressingEach method needs different film behaviour - guessing causes returns
SubstrateBoard type and grade the film will bond toOpacity and bond requirements follow the core
Annual volumeRealistic forecast, stated as forecast not commitmentVolume bands price differently; inflated forecasts poison the relationship
Release scheduleOrder cadence - one shipment or monthly releasesHolding stock for you has a cost; monthly release prices differently
Compliance documentsREACH / RoHS 2.0 / phthalate reports requiredDocumented, third-party-tested product is priced honestly, not cheaply
SamplesHow many designs, shipped whereSample logistics are quoted or absorbed - state it
IncotermsEXW, FOB, CIF or DDP with the destination portFreight math can be 10 percent of landed cost - the shipping guide works it
Quality standardReference master sample, batch tolerance, receiving checklistSuppliers price to the quality they are being held to

The physical sample reference is the single most valuable field in the RFQ - everything else hangs off it.

Declare the Application, Not Just the Product

Two buyers can order the same design at the same gauge for completely different jobs - a flat laminator needs flatness and bond behaviour, a membrane presser needs elongation, a wrapper needs conformability. A supplier who knows the application recommends the grade that survives it; a supplier who only knows the design quotes the cheapest stock that looks right. Declare the method and the substrate, and you will get engineering in the quote, not just a price.

If the programme includes edges, put the banding in the same RFQ - matched colour is a production decision, not an afterthought.

The Volume Trap

The most common RFQ error is quoting an annual volume the buyer cannot commit to, to fish for the volume price. What actually happens: the supplier prices to the forecast, holds capacity or stock, the real orders come in at half the forecast, and every subsequent negotiation starts with an argument. State the honest forecast and the honest release schedule - the price breakdown guide shows exactly where those numbers land in the quote.

Comparing the Quotes That Come Back

  1. Normalise to the same basis: price per square metre at the same gauge and width, or price per roll of the same length.
  2. Separate film, samples, tooling and freight - a low film price with a high freight line is not a low price.
  3. Check the compliance documents are named, attached and current.
  4. Check lead time is stated for the release schedule you declared, not a best case.
  5. Ask what is not in the quote - masters, batch holds, colour tolerance guarantees are real services that real suppliers price or provide.

The supplier-side checklist - what to verify about the factory behind the quote - is in the supplier vetting guide, and the landed-cost arithmetic with freight and MOQ is worked through in the shipping guide. Bring those two and this RFQ, and the quotes you get back will be worth comparing.
